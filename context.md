# Mountain Huts

*Context document — revised 22 October 2026*

## Setting

Mountain huts provide a point of hospitality and refuge for people travelling in mountain areas. They are often owned by alpine clubs or public authorities and operated by a warden with the support of hut employees. Their facilities are generally simple: shared sleeping spaces, communal dining areas, and basic food and drink services.

A hut may be a destination for a day trip, an overnight stop on a longer route, or a base from which visitors begin another hike. It also offers shelter when weather or other circumstances make continuing a journey difficult. Some huts provide additional facilities, such as e-bike charging, where local infrastructure allows.

Hospitality in this setting involves more than welcoming guests. Space, supplies, energy, and staff time are limited, while demand and visitors’ plans can change with weather and mountain conditions. Meal service can concentrate many arrivals into a short period, and staff must balance immediate requests with preparations for guests expected later.

## People and responsibilities

The hut brings together visitors planning or undertaking mountain activities and the people responsible for its daily operation. For this project, the following terms describe their relationship with the system:

- **User:** any person accessing the service’s public web pages.
- **Hiker:** a registered user interacting with the hut through the web application, either individually or on behalf of a party. The term also covers visitors using facilities such as e-bike charging.
- **Warden:** the person responsible for managing the hut and overseeing its services and operational decisions.
- **Employee:** a member of the hut team carrying out day-to-day work. The warden can also perform the functions available to employees.

A **party** is a group of people travelling or using hut services together. One hiker acts as the party’s point of contact with the system; the party itself is not a separate user role.

## Why a system is needed

Visitors need a dependable basis for planning their stop at the hut. Uncertainty about services or arrangements can affect the wider journey, particularly when alternatives are distant or plans change during a trip.

The hut team needs a consistent understanding of expected demand, available resources, and relevant guest information. Requests may reach staff through the web, by telephone or message, or in person. When information is scattered across conversations or separate records, staff can lose track of changes, misunderstand a party’s needs, or make commitments that exceed the hut’s capacity. Resolving those discrepancies also takes time away from serving guests.

The mountain setting gives some information an additional importance. Knowing a visitor’s intended journey and expected arrival can help staff assess an unexplained delay and provide useful context if follow-up is necessary. Such information supports staff judgement and established safety procedures; it cannot guarantee a visitor’s safety or replace rescue services.

## Purpose of the system

The proposed web application will support coordination between hikers and the hut team, providing a shared and current basis for organising hospitality services and relevant information about visitors’ planned activities.

Its value is to help visitors plan with greater confidence, help staff use limited resources effectively, and reduce misunderstandings as circumstances change. The application should fit the practical work of running a hut, where staff attention must remain available for guests and immediate operational needs.

This document establishes the setting, terminology, and reasons for the project. The accompanying user stories define the specific capabilities and business rules, and distinguish the proposed work from possible additions.
